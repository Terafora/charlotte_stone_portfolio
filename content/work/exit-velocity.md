## One project, many disciplines

Exit Velocity began as a way to learn VR development and grew into a broader Sega Saturn-inspired science-fiction racing project. It gives me one persistent world in which to explore programming, game design, visual art, music, character design and storytelling.

## The visual system

The original development blog used a fixed teal navigation rail, an animated sawtooth edge and a black information ticker. The forms deliberately felt a little mechanical and a little handmade—closer to a fictional broadcast interface than a conventional blog.

That visual language now has a second life in this portfolio: the rail and ticker remain, but the content has more space to breathe and the warmer Pastel Pocket palette softens the overall system.

## Why keep a project this broad?

The project is useful precisely because it exceeds one discipline. Each new constraint exposes a different gap: how input should feel, how a world communicates speed, how sound supports direction, or how a character can make a technical system emotionally legible.

## What I am learning

Long projects need a strong centre and loose edges. The centre explains why the thing should exist. The edges leave room for experiments that could never have been planned at the start.
