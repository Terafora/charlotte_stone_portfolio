## Safety lives in small decisions

Emotional safety can sound abstract until it meets an interface. Then it becomes very concrete: who can see a draft, whether progress can be saved, how an error is phrased, what the product remembers, and whether a person can back out without explanation.

## The data model has a tone of voice

If a product stores every abandoned attempt, ranks a person against others or exposes private practice by default, those are not neutral technical choices. They tell the user what kind of relationship the system expects.

Designing for dignity often means collecting less, making states reversible and explaining consequences before an action.

## Gentleness still needs clarity

A soft visual style cannot compensate for vague language. Reassuring interfaces work best when the action is precise: what will be shared, with whom, what remains private, and what happens next.

The goal is not to remove every difficult moment. It is to stop the interface from adding unnecessary difficulty of its own.
